Vijay Shekhar Sharma

From a Hindi-medium school near Aligarh to the founder of Paytm, the app that put payments on the phone for millions of Indians.

Born
7 June 1978, Aligarh, Uttar Pradesh, India
Known for
Founding One97 Communications and Paytm
Industry
Fintech, digital payments
Education
B.Tech, Delhi College of Engineering (now DTU)

Early life

Vijay Shekhar Sharma was born in Aligarh in 1978, the third of four children. His father, Sulom Prakash Sharma, was a schoolteacher and his mother, Asha Sharma, a homemaker. He went to a Hindi-medium school in Harduaganj, a small town near Aligarh, and started college at just 15. He graduated with a B.Tech from the Delhi College of Engineering, now Delhi Technological University, at 19.

Moving from a Hindi-medium school into an English-speaking engineering college was hard, and accounts of his life describe him working steadily on his English and his confidence rather than giving up.

First ventures

While still in college, in 1997, he started a website called indiasite.net. He sold it two years later for about US$1 million, a large sum for a student at the time. In 2000 he founded One97 Communications, which supplied mobile content such as news, cricket scores, ringtones, jokes and exam results to telecom customers. It was a modest business, but it taught him how mobile phones, telecom operators and everyday users actually behave.

The launch of Paytm

In 2010 One97 launched Paytm, a name often explained as “pay through mobile”. It began with prepaid mobile recharges and utility bill payments, which saved people a trip to a shop. Over the years it added a mobile wallet, QR-code payments for shopkeepers, merchant payment tools, ticketing, e-commerce and financial products such as insurance, investments and lending.

Demonetisation and rapid growth

The big turning point came in November 2016, when India withdrew 86% of its paper currency overnight. Millions of people suddenly needed another way to pay, and Paytm was ready. Forbes reported that its daily transactions tripled to about 8 million, and Vijay was named India's youngest billionaire. In August 2018, Berkshire Hathaway invested US$300 million in the company, a major endorsement for Indian fintech.

The IPO and the setbacks

In November 2021 Paytm went public, raising US$2.5 billion at a valuation of about US$19 billion, then India's largest IPO. The shares fell steeply afterwards. In early 2024 the Reserve Bank of India took action against Paytm Payments Bank, restricting its ability to take fresh deposits, and on 26 February 2024 Vijay stepped down as its part-time non-executive chairman and a board member. Critics pointed to governance and compliance gaps and to over-expansion into areas such as e-commerce and banking. The action was aimed at the bank, not at Vijay personally.

The turnaround

Paytm responded by refocusing on its core payments and merchant business and cutting costs. In 2024 it sold its movie ticketing and events business to Zomato. By the 2025–26 financial year the company reported its first full-year profit, reported at about ₹552 crore, which Vijay called “sustainably profitable”. For the June 2026 quarter it reported a profit of ₹220 crore on revenue of about ₹2,448 crore. He remains founder, chairman, managing director and CEO, and was reappointed as MD and CEO until December 2027.

Beyond Paytm

Vijay is an angel investor who has backed around 80 startups, including Hiver, GOQii, Zapr, NoBroker, Treebo and HackerEarth. He sits on the board of management of Netaji Subhas University of Technology in Delhi, was named UN Environment's Patron for Clean Air in 2017, and in 2022 became India's Universal Acceptance Ambassador for the ICANN-supported Universal Acceptance Steering Group. He is married to Mridula Parashar Sharma and they have one child.

Leadership lessons

  • Pay attention to the thing that frustrates you. A language barrier became something he worked to overcome.
  • Build on what you already have. A mobile-content company gave One97 the telecom know-how to become Paytm.
  • Be ready when the moment comes. Paytm was in place when demonetisation changed how India paid.
  • Growth in too many directions has a cost, and focusing on the core business helped Paytm return to profit.
  • Talk to your users directly, especially in a crisis. He often addresses customers and investors himself.
The takeaway: make the hard thing, paying, as easy as a tap, and adoption follows, but stay close to your core. Vijay's path from a Hindi-medium school to India's largest IPO, and then through a regulatory crisis back to profit, shows how persistence and focus can carry a company through both the highs and the lows.
